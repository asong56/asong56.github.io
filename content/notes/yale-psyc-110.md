## Lecture 0 | Introduction

Two competing frameworks for understanding the mind:

| Framework | View |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Intuitive Psychology** (Dualism) | Mind (soul) and body (physical matter) are distinct entities. |
| **Scientific Psychology** (Materialism) | All mental events are products of neuronal activity. |

## Lecture 1 | Foundations: The Neuron & Brain

### The Neuron

> You're nothing but a pack of neurons. — *Lewis Carroll's Alice*

- **All-or-None Law**: A neuron either fires a full electrical impulse or not at all — analogous to binary code.
- **Neurotransmitters**: Chemical messengers released into the synapse to communicate between cells.

### Brain Structure

| Region | Function |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Subcortical Structures** | Basic instincts and drives |
| **Cerebral Cortex** | Reasoning, language, perception |

- **Hemispheric Specialization**: Left brain → logic and language; right brain → spatial and holistic perception.
- **Split-Brain**: When the corpus callosum is severed, visual fields and movements lose coordination between hemispheres.

## Lecture 3 | Foundations: Freud

### The Structure of Mind

Freud saw personality as a constant conflict between three forces, all driven by **unconscious motivation**:

| Structure | Nickname | Principle | Role |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Id** | The Child | Pleasure Principle | Seeks immediate gratification (hunger, sex, aggression) |
| **Superego** | The Judge | Moral Principle | Internal compass learned from parents and society |
| **Ego** | The Mediator | Reality Principle | Satisfies the Id in ways acceptable to the Superego |

### Psychosexual Stages of Development

Freud argued personality is shaped by how libidinal energy is handled in childhood:

1. **Oral** — Focus on the mouth (sucking, eating).
2. **Anal** — Focus on control and cleanliness (toilet training).
3. **Phallic** — Focus on sexual identity; Oedipus complex. *(Penis envy occurs here in Freud's theory.)*
4. **Latency** — Sexual drives are repressed; a quiet developmental period.
5. **Genital** — Mature adult sexuality (puberty onward).

### Defense Mechanisms

The Ego uses these strategies to reduce anxiety from the Id–Superego conflict:

| Mechanism | Definition | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Displacement** | Redirecting impulses to a safer target | Angry at your father; kick the dog instead |
| **Projection** | Attributing your own thoughts to others | A man with sexual urges accuses others of thinking about sex |
| **Rationalization** | Constructing logical excuses for bad behavior | "I hit the child for their own good" |
| **Regression** | Reverting to childish behavior under stress | An adult throwing a tantrum |

### Scientific Criticism

- **Sexism**: Concepts like penis envy are widely regarded as culturally biased.
- **Unfalsifiability**: Freudian theories cannot be empirically tested or disproven — disqualifying them as scientific by modern standards.
- **The Legacy**: Despite these flaws, Freud correctly emphasized that the **unconscious mind** and **early childhood experiences** have profound influence on adult behavior.

## Lecture 4 | Foundations: Skinner & Behaviorism

### More on the Unconscious (Freud cont.)

Most mental activity is unconscious. This has evolutionary roots — humans are wired to:

- Deceive others to appear more favorable.
- Detect when others are deceiving them.
- Believe their own best lies (children who "truly believe" in their sadness give more authentic performances).

### Behaviorism

**Key figures**: John B. Watson · B. F. Skinner

**Core propositions**:
- All behavior is derived from learning — no innate nature.
- Only observable behavior matters; internal mental states are not studied.
- Humans and animals are fundamentally the same in learning mechanisms.

### Learning Mechanisms

1. **Habituation** — Learning to ignore irrelevant or repeated stimuli.

2. **Classical Conditioning** *(Ivan Pavlov)* — `neutral stimulus` + `unconditioned stimulus` → `conditioned response`
   - **Extinction**: Response fades when the conditioned stimulus is repeatedly presented without the unconditioned stimulus.
   - **Spontaneous Recovery**: Extinguished response reappears after a rest period.
   - **Generalization**: Response transfers to stimuli similar to the conditioned stimulus.

3. **Operant Conditioning** *(Skinner)* — Consequences shape future behavior.
   - Reward → behavior increases.
   - Punishment → behavior decreases.

   | Type | Definition | Example |
   | :--- | :--- | :--- |
   | **Positive Reinforcement** | Add a reward | Give a treat for sitting |
   | **Negative Reinforcement** | Remove something aversive | Turn off alarm when you wake up |
   | **Punishment** | Add something aversive | Fine for speeding |

   > **Note**: Negative Reinforcement ≠ Punishment. Negative reinforcement *increases* behavior by removing something unpleasant.

   - **Shaping**: Reinforcing successive approximations toward a target behavior.
   - **Partial Reinforcement**: Intermittent schedules produce more resistant behavior than continuous reinforcement.

### Criticisms of Behaviorism

Behaviorism is considered *too extreme* — it ignores cognition, emotion, and biological predispositions entirely.
