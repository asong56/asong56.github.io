# Worker and human-like AI are not the same optimization target

*Partially edited by AI*

The obvious framing is behavioral: a perfect worker is accurate and stable, a human-like AI is warm and unpredictable. But I think the gap is deeper.

A worker's model is built around task completion — maximize correct output, minimize errors, close every loop. A human's model is built around limited attention, emotional memory, and heuristic shortcuts. These're different internal structures.

What most AI companies are building right now is a system that tries to be both simultaneously. That feels like a category error.

The more interesting question: if you wanted to build something genuinely human-like, what would its *internal structure* look like? Not just the output distribution, but the actual reasoning process — the part that produces vague initial reactions before finding reasons for them.
