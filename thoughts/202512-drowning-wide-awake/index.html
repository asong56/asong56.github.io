I’m drowning, and I am wide awake.*

Jan 24, 2026 mood, insight

I’m drowning, and I am wide awake.

My quarter 2 GPA had fallen to 3.5 since I got ‘A-’ out of most of my classes and two ‘B’s. I got B+ on Precalculus because I didn’t get A on the final three tests. It’s so ridiculous that the person who has self-studied three college-level math courses failed high school Precalculus.

A journal on 12/2

My problem is that last night, I swore to myself, "no more novels," and then spent four hours reading one this morning.

My problem is that I hate rigid schedules; they suffocate me. So I spent an entire night trying to build the "perfect" flexible system in Notion, only to waste all my time on the tool instead of the work.

My problem is also that I'm a "burst" worker. When I'm in the zone, I can do three days' worth of work in two hours. But I always stop at 80%. I would rather turn in a fast, rough draft than spend time polishing it. That process is physically painful to me.

I'm not coasting in ignorance. I am drowning, and I am wide awake.

I know exactly where this comes from. It’s a habit I built in the 4th grade. I coasted on being "the smart kid"—I got into a top-tier middle school in Shanghai with only a month of half-assed studying. That just proved to me that I didn't need to try.

But now, in 9th grade, I've finally run out of "smart" to coast on. And I'm just watching it all happen.

Over Thanksgiving break, I started (another) rescue mission. I emailed my teachers to ask about my backed-up questions. I forced myself to finish my late CS50 assignments.

But these actions don't feel like a new beginning. They just feel like another battle in a long war against my own habits.

I don't know when, or if, I'll ever actually win. All I can do is admit my addiction and keep trying—even though I know I'll probably just open that novel again tomorrow morning.

I'm not trying to present myself as someone who "overcame" a problem.

I'm just trying to show you a real person who's in a five-year war with his own inertia. I see my own failures clearly, and I haven't given up on analyzing them or trying to change.

Just writing this down. At least it's honest.
